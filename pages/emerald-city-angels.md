<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Emerald City Angels is a junior guild supporting <a href="https://www.seattlechildrens.org/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Seattle Children&#8217;s Hospital</a>, and I serve as its Junior Guild President. It started with a summer crochet class and a simple idea &#8212; that a handmade hat is a small thing to give and a real thing to receive &#8212; and grew into a guild that raises money for the hospital and puts hats and blankets into the hands of its smallest patients.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Supporting research at Seattle Children&#8217;s</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The money our guild raises goes to Seattle Children&#8217;s, where a significant part of it supports <a href="https://www.seattlechildrens.org/research/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">pediatric research</a>. This is the part of the mission I care about most, and it is the same reason I want to go into medicine.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Treating a child who is sick today matters enormously. But research is what changes the odds for every child who arrives next year, and the year after that. A better protocol, a gentler treatment, an earlier diagnosis &#8212; these do not come from any single hospital visit. They come from years of careful work that no family&#8217;s bill ever pays for directly.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That gap is exactly what guilds exist to close. Research and uncompensated care are not funded by what insurers reimburse, so hospitals like Seattle Children&#8217;s depend on volunteer guilds to make up the difference. Emerald City Angels is one of many such guilds, and our contributions have gone toward research and pediatric care, medical equipment, and play therapy for children in treatment.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It is a strange and wonderful thing to realize that an afternoon of knitting, multiplied across enough people, becomes laboratory time.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How we raise it</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most of our fundraising happens through micro&#8209;volunteering &#8212; short, hands&#8209;on events that let busy people contribute without clearing a whole weekend. For two years running we have hosted the GiVE campaign at Microsoft&#8217;s campus, where I have taught software engineers to knit hats for premature babies. The click&#8209;clack of needles in a tech building is a sound I did not expect to love.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The model works because it stacks two kinds of giving. Volunteers donate hours and make something real with their hands, and many employers then match those hours with financial contributions. One evening of knitting turns into both a hat and a cheque.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What we&#8217;ve raised</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In October 2023, our first GiVE campaign raised roughly <strong>$20,000</strong> through a combination of volunteer hours and direct donations. In 2024 we raised over <strong>$15,000</strong> more, alongside a growing pile of handmade hats and blankets.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you would like to get involved &#8212; volunteering, donating, or simply passing the word along &#8212; I would love to hear from you.</p>
