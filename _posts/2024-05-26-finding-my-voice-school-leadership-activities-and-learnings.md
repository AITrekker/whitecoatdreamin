<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I&#8217;m a 10th grader who&#8217;s been really involved in our school&#8217;s activities. This year, I served on the <strong>inclusion and advisory committee </strong>for our school district. It was such a great experience working with others to make our school a better place for everyone. I also decided to take a big step and run for class officer and ASP treasurer. Spoiler alert: I didn&#8217;t win either election.</p>
<!--more-->




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Here&#8217;s what I learned from the experience:</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size">1. <strong>Stepping Out of My Comfort Zone</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Running for office was nerve-wracking, but I&#8217;m proud of myself for trying. It was a big leap, and that&#8217;s a win in itself.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">2. <strong>Handling Defeat</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Losing taught me to handle disappointment and keep going. It&#8217;s a chance to grow and improve, not a reflection of my worth.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">3. <strong>Gaining New Skills</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Campaigning helped me improve my communication, organization, and time management skills, which will be useful in the future.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">4. <strong>Building Relationships</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I understood the loyalty friendships can bring when all my friends supported me unconditionally. I met many new people and formed valuable connections during my campaign.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">5. <strong>Staying Involved</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Even without an official position, I&#8217;m committed to making a difference through the inclusion and advisory committee and other activities.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">To my fellow students, don&#8217;t hesitate to pursue your goals. Every experience helps you grow as a person.</p>
