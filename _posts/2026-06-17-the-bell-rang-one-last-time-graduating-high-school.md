<p class="wp-block-paragraph">People always say that the four years of high school fly by. For most of high school, I never completely understood what they meant.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Now that I have graduated, I do.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I still remember my first day of freshman year. I remember walking through the building, trying to understand my schedule, paper map in my hand, and hearing the bell ring after first period for the first time. I remember the morning announcements, the assemblies, and meeting people during that first semester who would later become some of my closest friends.</p>
<!--more-->




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">At the time, four years sounded incredibly long. Somehow, they are already over.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">A High School Experience of My Own</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I had attended private school for my entire life before high school, so entering a larger high school environment felt completely new.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most movies and TV shows portray high school as full of drama, bullying, and conflict, but my experience was much better. It was filled with friendships, laughter, assemblies, challenging classes, inside jokes, and ordinary moments that became meaningful over time.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Some days felt endless while I was living them. Looking back, they passed in seconds, and I would choose my real high school experience over the movie version every time.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">A Time of Firsts</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">High school was filled with firsts.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It was the first time I took AP classes, prepared for major standardized tests, became deeply involved in clinical programs, led a guild, applied to college, and had to advocate for myself academically.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It was also the first time I truly learned how to study. I discovered that doing well was not only about working longer. It was about understanding how I learned, organizing my time, changing strategies when something was not working, and being willing to ask questions.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most importantly, I learned self-advocacy. I became more comfortable speaking with my teachers, asking for clarification, requesting opportunities, and communicating when I needed help.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">A Year of Lasts</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Senior year is also a time filled with lasts.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There was the last first day of school, the last Homecoming, the last football game, the last school assembly, the last round of morning announcements, and the last time walking through those hallways.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">More than anything, senior year taught me to live in the moment. I tried to say yes more, take on new challenges, and enjoy experiences without always worrying about what came next. You only get to live each chapter once.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And perhaps one of the most satisfying lasts of all: AP exams are officially over forever!</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">How Much Has Changed</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The person graduating is very different from the freshman who walked into the building four years ago.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I have become more confident, independent, resilient, and willing to speak up for myself. I have learned how to handle disappointment, celebrate progress, manage pressure, and keep moving even when things do not go according to plan.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">High school was not perfect, but it was mine. Everything that happened was part of the experience.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Looking Ahead</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Graduation is strange because it feels like both an ending and a beginning.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I am sad to leave behind the people, routines, and places that have been part of my life for four years. At the same time, I am excited to move to Los Angeles, and continue my journey toward medicine.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The four years really did fly by. I just did not realize how quickly until I was standing at the end of them.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The bells, announcements, assemblies, classes, and exams may be over, but the lessons and memories will stay with me as I step onto the road ahead.</p>
